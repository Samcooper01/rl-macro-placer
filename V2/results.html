rl-macro-placer, V2 results

A faithful reduced version of the Google graph-placement method: reinforcement learning places the macros, a force-directed pass places the standard-cell clusters, and a three-term proxy reward scores the whole placement.

The pipeline (per episode)

  1. The RL (Reinforcement Learning) agent places the hard macros one at a time (sized rectangles; action masking forbids overlap).
  2. The soft standard-cell clusters are placed by a force-directed method around the fixed macros (springs pull connected objects together, repulsion spreads them out).
  3. The reward is the negative weighted sum of three cheap proxies over the full placement: wirelength (HPWL, Half-Perimeter WireLength), congestion (a RUDY, Rectangular Uniform wire DensitY, estimate), and density.

This mirrors the paper, where the true cost (full place-and-route) is far too slow to run inside millions of RL episodes, so a fast proxy is used. The three terms trade off against each other: the agent cannot simply clump the macros (that raises congestion and density) or simply spread them (that raises wirelength).
